Success at all costs is the modus operandi for biological organisms. I am reminded of this powerful demonstration by the Kishony lab as to how bacteria can adjust to their environment to break through containment in order to live. In this case, they adapt to ever-increasing concentrations of antibiotics, from lethal to bacteria, to 10 times that amount, then 100 times, and then 1,000 times. Note how the breakthrough events occur.

I am very excited about the use of AI to perform complex tasks, but much like in my field of molecular biology, when biologists learned how to manipulate genes back in the 1970s, the world was concerned about manipulating the "code of life." In response, the molecular biology community came together and held a now-famous [Asilomar conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asilomar_Conference_on_Recombinant_DNA). This conference helped to establish the ground rules for going forward with the newfound powerful technology. Are we not at the same point with AI? We need an Asilomar for AI, and importantly, we need more from the broader community and not just the companies driving this rapidly evolving technology.

The above video shows what "containment" looks like and how a species or an agent can behave when success at all costs drives the behavior.
